## The Question

When people find out I'm pursuing product management *and* actively building software, the reaction is usually some version of: *"Why? You don't need to code to be a PM."*

They're right. You don't. But I think they're asking the wrong question.

## What Building Actually Teaches You

The real value of being able to build isn't that you can write production code for your engineering team. It's that you develop an intuition for **what's hard and what's easy** — and that intuition changes every product conversation you have.

When you've built an authentication system yourself, you stop treating "just add Google login" as a two-hour task. When you've designed a database schema, you understand why a seemingly small feature request might require a migration. When you've shipped a PWA, you know what "offline support" actually costs.

That knowledge makes you a better partner. Not a better programmer.

## My Path

I didn't start as a developer. I spent five years in operations — managing teams, analyzing data, optimizing processes. Then I invested a year learning to build: HTML, CSS, JavaScript, React, Node.js, databases, APIs. Full stack.

Then I built things. A word game (Hangman) to learn DOM manipulation and UX feedback loops. A book search engine (MERN + GraphQL) to understand API architecture. A productivity app (drivn) to put product thinking into practice end-to-end.

Each project taught me something a course couldn't: what it actually feels like to ship something and have it not work the way you expected.

## The Hybrid Advantage

The best PMs I've learned from all share one trait: they make their team's job easier. They come into planning with technically grounded ideas. They don't spec out impossible things. They can look at a PR and broadly understand what changed and why.

That's the PM I'm building toward. Not the one who replaces the engineers — but the one the engineers trust to understand them.

## Why I'll Keep Building

Even in a PM role, I'll keep building side projects. Not because I'll write the product code — but because staying close to the craft keeps the intuition sharp. You can't develop empathy for your engineering team from a distance.

drivn is live. This portfolio is live. There's more coming.

That's why I build.
