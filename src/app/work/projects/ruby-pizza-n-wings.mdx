## Overview

Ruby Pizza N' Wings is a family-run pizza and wings restaurant at 162 Main St
in Toronto's Upper Beaches. They asked me for a website — what they actually
needed was a digital presence they owned. This project covers both sides of
that: the shipped three-page site, and the business analysis and product
ownership work that shaped it.

[View on GitHub →](https://github.com/VithusanU/rubyPizzaNWings)

**Timeline:** Site started at the restaurant's request in July 2023 →
business analysis engagement through 2024 → completed build-out and formal
documentation in July 2026.

## The Client's Problem

The restaurant's entire digital presence was a third-party Uber Eats listing:

- **Commission on every order** — no channel promoting direct phone orders or
  free pickup
- **No brand control** — the listing couldn't show the storefront, the story,
  or the branded promo artwork the owner had already paid for
- **A hidden differentiator** — butter chicken pizza, shawarma plates, and
  souvlaki set the menu apart from every other pizza shop on the strip, and
  nothing surfaced it

## What I Delivered

### The website

A responsive, no-build static site (HTML/CSS/vanilla JS) that any host can serve:

- **Home** — hero over the storefront photo, address/hours/click-to-call info
  strip, fan favourites, and family deal cards using the client's own promo artwork
- **Menu** — the full 13-category menu with every item priced, sourced from the
  live Uber Eats listing, with category jump navigation and "most liked" badges
- **About Us** — the restaurant's story, positioned around its international menu
- Every page keeps two ordering paths one tap away: phone (`tel:` links) and
  the Uber Eats store

### The BA/PO documentation

The part most small-business sites skip. Four working documents live in the
repo's [`docs/`](https://github.com/VithusanU/rubyPizzaNWings/tree/main/docs) folder:

- **[Business Requirements Document](https://github.com/VithusanU/rubyPizzaNWings/blob/main/docs/01-business-requirements.md)**
  — problem statement, five measurable business objectives, stakeholder
  matrix, scope in/out, assumptions, constraints, and a risk register
- **[Functional Requirements Specification](https://github.com/VithusanU/rubyPizzaNWings/blob/main/docs/02-functional-requirements.md)**
  — 21 numbered functional requirements and 7 NFRs, each traceable to what
  shipped, plus open items for the owner (like price parity between in-store
  and delivery-platform menus)
- **[User Stories & Product Backlog](https://github.com/VithusanU/rubyPizzaNWings/blob/main/docs/03-user-stories-backlog.md)**
  — three customer personas, five epics tied to business objectives, delivered
  stories with Given/When/Then acceptance criteria, and a MoSCoW-prioritized
  future backlog
- **[Release Plan & Roadmap](https://github.com/VithusanU/rubyPizzaNWings/blob/main/docs/04-release-plan.md)**
  — v1.0 shipped with honest known limitations; v1.1 (hosting, SEO, analytics)
  and v1.2 (conversion) planned; v2.0 on-site ordering explicitly gated on
  traffic data rather than speculation

## Decisions Worth Noting

- **Ship static, defer the backend.** No budget, one maintainer, content that
  changes rarely — a static site the owner can host anywhere beat a CMS the
  owner would never log into.
- **Treat Uber Eats as the source of truth.** The menu was populated from the
  live listing and date-stamped, turning "is this menu current?" from a
  guess into a checkable fact.
- **Gate the expensive ideas on data.** On-site ordering (the feature that
  kills commissions) sits in v2.0 behind an explicit analytics threshold —
  the roadmap says what evidence would justify building it.
- **Reuse the client's real assets.** The branded chalkboard promo cards
  became the site's design system: same colours, same artwork, print and web
  finally matching.

## Tech Stack

- **Frontend:** HTML5, CSS3 (flexbox/grid, mobile-first), vanilla JavaScript
- **Design source:** client's branded promo-card artwork
- **Data source:** live Uber Eats store listing (date-stamped retrieval)
- **Docs:** Markdown BRD / FRS / backlog / release plan in-repo
